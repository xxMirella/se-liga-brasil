At Itaú, I modernized a script used to calculate team budgets and balances.
Accuracy was critical because the output contained financial information used by the business.

While rebuilding the processing in PySpark, I found discrepancies between the new calculations and the original results.
Instead of assuming the old script was correct, I investigated the differences.
Several business rules were undocumented, and some were missing from the code entirely.

I worked with a business analyst for at least two hours a day to clarify those rules, compare historical results,
and identify errors in the original script. I also added checks for missing partition values,
appropriate financial data types, and coverage of all required business units for the semester.

Those daily working sessions helped me communicate progress and challenges through concrete examples:
which calculations matched, where discrepancies remained, and which rules needed clarification.
I documented the rules as we resolved them.

The final process included data quality checks and ran automatically when inputs arrived in AWS.
Processing time fell from 90 minutes to 10 minutes. The main lesson was that validating financial data requires
understanding the intended business rules, not simply reproducing legacy results.
